The snake and the box

induced paths in the hypercube

The condition

A snake is a path through the cube's vertices in which no two vertices are adjacent unless they are consecutive on the path. Every step flips one bit, and no step may bring you back within one bit of anywhere you have already been. Each vertex the snake takes burns its n neighbours, and a burnt vertex is gone for good. The snake eats its own room to move, which is why it stops long before it runs out of cube.

Where it stops being known

The search on this page settles n ≤ 5 in a few milliseconds. The same search reaches n = 6 in about 78 million nodes. Past that it needs symmetry reduction, canonical forms and distributed computation; n = 8 was closed only in 2014, after months of machine time. At n = 9 the longest snake anyone has built has 190 edges, and nobody has shown there is nothing longer.

Bounds

Abbott and Katchalski proved a lower bound of the form λ·2ⁿ with λ ≈ 0.3: however large the cube gets, a snake always occupies a constant fraction of it. Zémor proved an upper bound of the form 2ⁿ(1 − c/√n): it never occupies nearly all of it. The fill figure in the readout starts at 100 per cent and drops as you climb. Between those two results it has somewhere to settle, rather than dwindling to nothing.

Why anyone wanted this

Read the bit strings along a snake and you have a Gray code: one bit changes per step. Because the path is induced, a single flipped bit either moves you one place along the snake or throws you clean off it. Off it is not a codeword, so the fault announces itself instead of passing as a different valid reading. That is what sent people looking for long induced paths — position encoders that would rather report an error than a plausible lie.

Colophon

Snakes are computed in your browser, never stored. Vertex colour follows P7 phosphor: a blue-white strike that decays to a yellow-green afterglow, which is exactly what a burnt vertex is. The strip on the left is five-track paper tape with the sprocket holes between tracks two and three, punched as far as the read head.
